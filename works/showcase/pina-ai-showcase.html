The problem · who it's for

Estimators spend their day re-typing what's already in the CRM.

The CRM already holds the whole estimating machine: calibrated drawings, measured markups, assemblies, a price book and estimates. Turning a finished takeoff into a draft estimate is still copy-and-paste work, and so are the follow-up emails, the deadline checks and the morning catch-up.

An assistant could do that prep. But an AI that can email clients or change a live bid is a risk no contractor should take on trust. One confidently wrong square footage can lose a job.

PinaAi is built around that worry. Agents read freely and do the preparation. Anything that would send, change or create something becomes a card that waits for a person.

Estimators
Get a draft estimate from their own takeoff, with every gap called out.
Sales & acquisition
Get first replies, follow-ups and a daily brief drafted for them.
Owners
Decide what each agent may see, cap the monthly spend and see every run.
New users
Have a two-minute "How this works" page and buttons that say exactly what they do.
Rule 1

Reading is free. Doing needs a yes.

Agents look things up on their own. Every email, note or estimate they prepare waits in Approvals.

Rule 2

The agent never measures.

Quantities only come from takeoff markups a person drew on a sheet a person calibrated. A sheet with no scale is reported, not guessed.

Rule 3

The price stays with you.

Rates are suggested from the company price book. Approving never overwrites a price an estimator already set.

Takeoff estimator

A draft estimate, grouped the way an estimator reads it.

The estimator agent reads the takeoff a person measured, applies the same assemblies and waste factors as the CRM, and proposes lines. Approving it writes a draft estimate into the CRM's own estimate tables.

  • Every line says where it came from: measured, or not measured and why
  • Stale check: if a measurement moves before approval, the approval fails loudly
  • Assumptions written out for the estimator to confirm

Ask anything

Ask a question, get an answer or a card.

Chat runs a real agent on the server. A question gets a read-only answer. A request to draft something puts an approval card right inside the conversation. If a run fails, it says why in plain English and does not invent an answer.

  • Answers cite the runs behind them
  • Drafts arrive as cards, never as sent mail

The user flow

From a drawing set to a draft estimate in the CRM.

People do the measuring and the pricing. The agent does the reading and the paperwork in between. Each step says who does it.

  1. STEP 1

    Upload the set

    Pick the bid and upload its drawing set.

    Estimator
  2. STEP 2

    Sort the sheets

    PinaAi sorts the set by title and flags the sheets with no scale.

    Agent, then a check by the estimator
  3. STEP 3

    Measure

    The estimator calibrates and measures in the CRM's takeoff workspace.

    Estimator, in the CRM
  4. STEP 4

    Run the estimator

    The agent reads the takeoff and the price book and drafts the lines.

    Agent
  5. STEP 5

    Review the card

    Approve, edit, or reject with a reason the agent reads next time.

    Waits for a person
  6. STEP 6

    Price and send

    The draft lands in the CRM estimate. The estimator sets final prices.

    Estimator, in the CRM

What's next

Start with one agent, prove it, then widen.

The plan is to switch on the takeoff estimator first and run it alongside an estimator on live bids before anyone relies on it.

Next

Go live with one agent

Apply the schema from the CRM, switch the model on, and run the takeoff estimator next to a person on real bids.

After that

Mail and documents

Connect email, files and calendar through the CRM's existing Microsoft sign-in.

Planned

Lead tools

One-button jobs for leads, built the same way as the estimating tools, with every change queued for approval.

Planned

Team view

Let managers see their team's agents and runs, using the same rules as the CRM.
