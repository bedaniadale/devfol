The problem · who it's for

Two businesses, one team, too many tools.

Pina runs a real-estate brokerage and a plastering contractor, AP Plastering, out of the same office. The same people chase seller leads in the morning and price stucco bids in the afternoon.

Before the platform, each part of the work lived somewhere else: leads in a paid CRM, escrow deadlines in email threads, MLS math in spreadsheets, bids in PDFs, payroll in another sheet. Nothing linked a lead to the deal it became, or a bid to the crew who built it.

Half the team works from the Philippines on a California business day. Every extra login and every slow screen costs them hours, so the app was built to open instantly from cache and stay quick across the Pacific.

Acquisition reps
Work a call queue, text from the app, and never let a new lead wait.
Transaction coordinators
Keep every deal on its contract dates from offer to close of escrow.
Estimators & PMs
Take off drawings, price bids, schedule crews and bill progress.
Owners & admins
See pipeline, accountability and payroll across both businesses.

AP Plastering bids & takeoff

Measure the drawings, price the bid, win the job.

Each bid moves through six stages, from setup to awarded or lost. Estimators open the plans in a full takeoff workspace, calibrate the scale and trace walls, and the quantities feed the estimate.

  • Takeoff tools for area, linear feet, counts, clouds and callouts
  • Assemblies turn one traced wall into lath, stucco and trim lines
  • GC outreach and bid analytics with real loss reasons

The user flow

A lead's path from first call to closed deal.

The steps below follow one seller lead through the real-estate side. Every step happens inside the same record, so nothing is retyped.

  1. STEP 1

    Lead arrives

    A seller fills in the intake form. The lead is scored and assigned by round-robin within seconds.

    Leads · intake form
  2. STEP 2

    Rep calls first

    It appears at the top of My Day with a speed-to-lead timer. Call and text go out through the company line.

    My Day · Quo Phone
  3. STEP 3

    Property analyzed

    The house is run as a flip, rental or wholesale deal, and a max offer is set.

    MLS · Analysis tab
  4. STEP 4

    Offer becomes a deal

    On acceptance the offer converts to a deal. Parties, price and dates carry over.

    MLS · Offers → Deals
  5. STEP 5

    Escrow tracked

    Contract deadlines, waiting-on items and client updates run until close of escrow.

    Deals · Transaction tab
  6. STEP 6

    Results counted

    The closing rolls into Reports, source ROI and each rep's accountability score.

    Reports · Accountability
Screens

On the phone, too.

The app runs as a phone app with a bottom bar and a More hub. Lists turn into cards and dialogs become bottom sheets.

UI kit in context

One look, taken from the transaction tab.

Every screen shares one small kit: bordered section cards on a white canvas, borderless inset rows, soft tone pills and text-link actions. No shadows, and nothing heavier than medium weight.
