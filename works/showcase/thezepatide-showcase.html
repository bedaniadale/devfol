A clinic website that sends every visitor to a doctor, not a checkout.

TheZepatide offers compounded, prescription-only weight-loss and wellness therapies. Its website has to earn trust, show the products honestly and get people into a free medical assessment. The clinic also has to be able to edit every word of it themselves.

The brief

Prescription-only products, sold with restraint.

The clinic had an older Laravel site. I rebuilt it in React with Supabase behind it. First I ported the original copy, look and behaviour exactly, then redesigned it. The products are compounded injectables, so the site can't behave like an online shop.

Visitors

Mostly on phones

People coming in from Facebook and Messenger, often comparing clinics and unsure whether a therapy suits them.

The ask

Trust before the button

Who oversees the care, what each therapy is, and who it isn't for, all shown before anyone is asked to commit.

Constraint

No prices, no cart

Every therapy is prescription-gated. Each path ends in the free assessment or a chat with the clinical team.

Constraint

The clinic edits it

Products, results, videos, team, contact details and section copy all come from a website editor the clinic runs.

Key features

The page, section by section.

Each section answers the next question a visitor has, in order: what this is, whether it's safe for them, whether it works, who's behind it and how to start.

Hero

One product, in focus.

“Prandex— available only through The Zepatide.”

The signature therapy stands on a lit pedestal and turns in 3D, with the other vials blurred behind it. The light follows the pointer. The claims sit in the still column beside the vial. On a rotating label they'd be unreadable for most of the turn.

  • The 3D vial (three.js) loads after first paint, so it never holds up the page.
  • Under the buttons: “Free, no obligation,” with what actually happens next.
thezepatide.com/#home
Signature range

Three therapies we are known for.

NEXAVION™, GLYVION™ and TRIAVION™ each get a full row: a large vial photo, a plain definition, four key features and “Rx only · MD supervised.” The Korean product names are kept, the way they appear on the vials.

The clinic chooses which products appear here, in the website editor's “Signature picks.”

thezepatide.com/#signature
How it starts · Suitability & safety

Is this appropriate for you?

Three steps come first: take the free assessment, the medical team reviews you, then you start the guided programme. Then the safety section says plainly: “These therapies are not right for everyone.”

The precautions (thyroid history, GI effects, pancreatitis) are pulled from the notes attached to each product. They stay in step with the catalogue without anyone copying them over.

thezepatide.com/#safety
Real results

Shown unedited.

Two rows of patient photos and messages scroll in opposite directions. Tap one to open it full size. The clinic adds and removes them in the editor.

“Individual results vary and are not typical or guaranteed.”

That disclaimer sits right under the photos, not hidden in the footer.

thezepatide.com/#results
Product collection · Details drawer

Every product, no price tag.

The collection grid opens into a details drawer: Definition (open by default), Benefits, Precautions and Medical Supervision, one at a time. The only buttons at the bottom are Begin Assessment and Ask on Messenger.

  • Each card says “MD supervised,” and each vial is marked Rx only.
  • On a phone the drawer becomes a full-height sheet.
thezepatide.com/#products
Certificates

Lab results for actual clients.

Certificates for Endotoxin, Purity and Heavy Metals have their own page behind a passcode. The clinic gives the passcode only to ongoing clients. It stays unlocked for the rest of the visit, so moving between the site and the gallery doesn't ask again.

thezepatide.com/certificates
Team · Telemed

Your care has names behind it.

Placed after the products and results, when the visitor's question becomes “who is actually behind this?” The section shows the physician and the founder with their portraits, credentials and bios. Below them are what to expect and a dated telemed offer: “Now accepting clients for telemed consultation.”

thezepatide.com/#physician
Get in touch · Footer

Answered by nurses, not a call centre.

Messenger opens as a scannable QR code on desktop. Phone and email have copy buttons. Clinic hours are listed, and the footer shows whether the clinic is open right now (“Open now · until 10 PM”).

The footer repeats the main action: “Start with a free assessment.”

thezepatide.com/#about

On a phone

Where most visitors actually are.

Every section reflows to one column with 44px touch targets. The menu becomes a full sheet, and the Take Assessment button stays in the header.

The visitor's path

From a Facebook link to a medical review.

Every path through the site ends in one of two places: the free assessment, or a conversation with the clinical team.

  1. Land on the hero

    See the signature therapy, the three claims and “Free, no obligation.”

    #home

  2. Learn what it is

    Browse the signature range and open any product for its definition, benefits and precautions.

    #signature · #products

  3. Check suitability

    Read the three steps and the safety notes before committing to anything.

    #safety

  4. See proof & people

    Unedited results, the doctor and founder by name, lab certificates for clients.

    #results · #physician

  5. Take the assessment

    The medical team reviews it before anything is prescribed or dispensed. Or message the clinic first.

    Take Assessment · Let's Chat

Also built for the clinic

The patient app behind “Patient Portal”.

The Patient Portal button in the site header opens a client-monitoring app I built alongside the site. Clients log their weight and days on their phones. Nurses work the day's injection round from one list. The clinic sees who needs a follow-up. It uses the same brand, database and sign-in as the site.
