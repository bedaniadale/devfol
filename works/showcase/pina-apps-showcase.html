The problem

The CRM lives on a desk. The work doesn't.

Pina's CRM runs the office: leads, deals, bids, crews and payroll. But estimators walk job sites, crews start work at 6 am, and agents meet sellers at the kitchen table. None of them want a full CRM on a phone.

AP Plastering's owner sketched what the field needed in a single-page concept: write a bid on the walk, turn it into a scope of work, bill it by milestone, and see who is on which job. The office should see all of it without anyone retyping it later.

Pina Apps is that concept, built properly. Each app is one job done well on a phone, and every save writes to the same records the office already uses.

Estimators
Price a stucco job while standing in front of it, then send the scope of work as a PDF.
Project managers
Track won jobs, holds and change orders, and bill each milestone when it's done.
Agents & reps
Log a lead from a business card, run a property's numbers, answer texts.
Admins
Decide who gets which app. People only see the apps they were given.

AP PLASTERING · BIDS

Type the walk. Get a scope of work.

The estimator types what they see, like "14 windows, 2 columns, 800 sf stucco", and the app turns it into priced scope items. Labor, man-days and the payment schedule follow from the same entry.

  • Quick entry reads English and Spanish trade terms into 16 scope categories
  • Standard milestones: Mobilization 10%, Lath 25%, Scratch 30%, Brown 25%, Finish 10%
  • Scope of work PDF with proposal numbers the CRM shares, like 26-0418

AP PLASTERING · PROJECTS & INVOICES

Win it, then bill it a milestone at a time.

Every bid sits in Waiting, Won or Lost. Bids sent from the office show up too, tagged "From CRM". When a job is won, the Invoices tab lists its milestones as Upcoming, Ready to Bill or Invoiced.

  • On hold with an estimated restart date, so the schedule stays honest
  • Change orders add or deduct, and approved ones roll into the contract
  • One invoice per milestone, numbered like 26-0418-2, never billed twice

FLOOR PLANS · DESKTOP

Sketch it on site, then attach it to the CRM.

Draw rooms, walls, doors and windows to scale in feet. Shared walls are drawn once, areas add up as you go, and the finished sheet attaches to a property, lead or deal in one tap. On a desktop the same app opens full width with a dark side rail.

  • Keyboard tools that match the CRM takeoff: R room, D door, N window, M measure
  • Multiple floors, fixtures and notes on one plan
  • Export a clean PNG sheet, saved to the record's Files

The user flow

From the site walk to the last payment.

One stucco job through AP Plastering — Field & Bid. The office sees each step in the CRM as it happens.

  1. 01

    Open the app

    Sign in with the CRM login. My Apps shows only the tools you were given.

    My Apps
  2. 02

    Walk the job

    Type what you see. Scope items, labor and milestones fill in.

    Bids · quick entry
  3. 03

    Send the scope

    Preview and send the scope of work PDF. The bid appears in the CRM's bid pipeline.

    Bids · PDF
  4. 04

    Mark it won

    The award creates the job in the CRM with its contract amount and scope.

    Projects
  5. 05

    Schedule the crew

    Put the job on the calendar and assign plasterers from the roster.

    Schedule
  6. 06

    Bill as you build

    Invoice each milestone as it's done, plus any signed change orders.

    Invoices
